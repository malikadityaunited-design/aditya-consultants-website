A BROADER POINT OF VIEW

Technical discipline.
Business in the real world.

Dr Malik holds a Master’s degree and a PhD from Newcastle University. His professional experience brings together technical training and hands-on business work in retail, IT, e-commerce, business management, consultancy, marketing, hospitality, finance, shipping and logistics across India, the United Kingdom, Germany, Italy and Hong Kong. His experience also includes working with Hutchison and running Scoopalicious Pvt Ltd.

His marine engineering background includes exposure to pipeline engineering and shipbuilding, alongside teaching and research connected with Newcastle University. That mix of technical and commercial perspectives informs how he approaches business questions: understand the context, look at the available evidence, and make the assumptions behind a decision visible.

He has supported startup founders and individuals as they work through business setup, market research and early operating challenges. Conversations can cover how to assess a market, which assumptions need testing, what may be practical in a particular context, and what alternatives deserve a closer look. After the initial conversations, Dr Malik works with each client to devise a personalised plan and roadmap, setting out priorities, practical actions and the support that fits their goals.

WHERE A CONVERSATION CAN HELP

Make the next step clearer.

Each discussion starts with your situation. The aim is to bring structure to the questions in front of you, make trade-offs easier to see and leave you with a clearer set of options. As the initial conversations build a fuller picture, we work with you on a personalised plan and roadmap for what to do next.

01

Starting or setting up

Clarify the idea, customer, operating model and early priorities. Identify which setup questions need specialist support.

02

Understanding a market

Explore customers, competitors and market conditions through focused research and practical questions before committing resources.

03

Testing what could work

Examine assumptions, consider alternatives and identify what may need more evidence, including what may not work as expected.

04

Navigating what comes next

Bring structure to operating and growth challenges across sectors such as retail, IT, marketing, hospitality and finance.
